Watch the network read a Poincaré map

Every segmentation on this page is the actual output of the paper's networks (instance, semantic and binary), computed offline on the inputs shown. Add orbits with the slider and see the picture sharpen; on the standard map, turn up the kick and watch the islands break up. Hover anything to link it across the views.

Input: orbits as the network sees them
One colour per orbit. Hover an orbit to see where the network put it.
Instance embedding 2-D projection (PCA) of the 10-D vectors
The instance network maps every regular pixel to a 10-D vector; pixels of one island chain land close together and MeanShift turns the clusters into instances. Up to 900 regular pixels are shown, coloured by the instance they were assigned to. Chaotic pixels are removed beforehand by the network's foreground head.
What the network found click a row or a region to pin it

How these were made. Each input is painted exactly as in training: consecutive iterates per orbit, binned on a 256 × 256 grid, every orbit in its own colour code. The orbits are nested, so the picture with more orbits contains every orbit of the picture with fewer. Instance: headline network, foreground head at ½, MeanShift with bandwidth 1.4, no size filter, the decoding of the paper's tables. Binary: headline binary network, threshold ½. Semantic: headline semantic network, trained and shown on the torus only. Scores are for this one map and painting; the paper's numbers pool all validation maps.

Standard map. p' = p + K sin x, x' = x + p' on the torus, with x across and p up; the same 200 random initial conditions for every K. It has no ground truth here, but hovering an orbit shows its finite-time Lyapunov exponent over the painted window as an independent check.
